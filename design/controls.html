Design system · controls

Every control a generated form can use.

Each control is live: type into the fields, open and search the listboxes, tick the boxes, pick files and record a voice note. Where a control has states you cannot reach by hand, a picker above it walks it there.

Every control is a shell around a bare native element. The shell is a span that carries the drawn boundary, the fill and the padding; the real input, textarea or select sits inside it with no border or background of its own. Focus, hover, disabled and invalid are declared once on the shell, and a chevron, unit or counter sits inside its boundary.

One field per row, with a grip beside it and a remove inside it; the order is the value. Drag the grip, or focus it and press Space, move with the arrows, Space to put down and Escape to put back; Tab or a press elsewhere drops it. The held row follows the pointer and the rows it passes slide aside, and the order changes when you let go. The last row empties instead of going away.

A closed set of values. The field carries its own values, and the capability declares whether it renders as a picker, a radio group or a segmented control. A picker supports groups, a note under an option, and an option that cannot be chosen.

A card shows a file and the open record plays, opens or downloads it. A video's card is its frame with its kind under it, a sound's says it holds one, and a document's names the file without its extension.

The second video draws no first frame, as iOS Safari shows every video; the well and the words under it carry the card.

The form only previews a video or a sound. Open takes the window to the record's render view: the file's player under a back control, with its name, size and download link below. Leaving a form or a build

Anything that would take away a form with unsaved changes asks first: putting the window away, another logo, Back and Forward, a prompt, Delete on a logo's menu, another record, the form's own Back and Cancel. A change is an edited field, or a file uploading or uploaded and not yet saved. Focus lands on the first answer, which changes nothing; Leave without saving lets the changes go, files included, then does what was pressed. A form nobody changed, changed back, or already saving closes without asking. Leaving a running build asks the same way, and stopping it gives back what the window held. Delete on a logo's menu asks over the capability's own collection, which stays dimmed and cannot be pressed.
